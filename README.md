# Marswalk — Jezero EVA route planner (MVP)

Interactive 3D map for planning one surface EVA at Jezero crater's delta front: pick a destination, compare three routes (Shortest / Gentlest / Science), then drag the mission timeline to watch two astronauts walk the route while the environment panel replays temperature, pressure, wind, dust and radiation for that place and time. Every number carries a provenance label (Observation / Historical Replay / Model Estimate / Assumption / Scenario Event). Glossary: [docs/CONTEXT.md](docs/CONTEXT.md).

> Live site: https://hmei3-hash.github.io/NASA_space_apps_challenge_2026/ (add `?demo` for the 30-second demo).

## Impact

Marswalk lets a Mars crew plan their own surface excursions. From NASA terrain, imagery, weather and radiation data it generates candidate routes, judges each one against suit mobility, slope, EVA time and accumulated dose, and labels every number by where it came from: measured, replayed, modelled or assumed. Given a surface weather-station network, it turns a dust-front detection into a countdown and immediately recomputes where the crew can shelter in time. In first person, the same numbers ride on a helmet visor.

The design scales past one EVA. If every crew, rover and habitat publishes what it observes to a shared hazard log, each becomes a sensor for the others: a team that sees a dust front arrive warns the teams downwind before it reaches them. For mission architects the tool is a trade-study sandbox: move stations and habitats, replay a storm, and see how a layout turns into minutes of warning, so the network can be planned before the first crew lands.

What this is not: there is no such station network on Mars today, the shared hazard log is a simulated design rather than a deployed service, and station layouts are compared by replaying a modelled front, not learned from mission data.

## Run

```powershell
npm install
npm run dev          # http://localhost:5173
npm test             # routing sanity tests on the synthetic terrain
```

With no data downloaded the app runs on a **clearly labeled synthetic terrain**. Real data replaces it automatically once `public/data/terrain.json` exists.

**30-second demo:** press **▶ Demo** in the header (Esc stops it), or open `http://localhost:5173/?demo` to start it automatically. It replays the 2018 storm on the globe, flies in to Jezero, picks the Science route, walks in first person, gets the dust warning from Team B, shelters in the rover, drives home and watches the storm pass. It drives the real app; nothing in it is pre-rendered.

## Get real data (PowerShell)

```powershell
powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File scripts\download_data.ps1          # terrain DEM + MEDA sol + raw images
conda create -n marswalk -c conda-forge python=3.11 gdal numpy -y
conda activate marswalk
python scripts\build_places.py data_raw\M20_waypoints.json
python scripts\preprocess_terrain.py --dem data_raw\M20_JezeroCrater_CTXDEM_20m.tif --ortho data_raw\JEZ_ctx_orthoMosaic_6m.tif --fit-places public\data\places.json --overwrite-sites
python scripts\preprocess_meda.py data_raw\meda\sol_0300 --sol 300
```

|---|---|---|
| Elevation / slope | Mars 2020 CTX DEM mosaic, 20 m (USGS Astrogeology / JPL) | Observation |
| Optical | Mars 2020 TRN CTX orthomosaic, 6 m (HiRISE 25 cm also works via `--ortho`, much larger) | Observation |
| Temperature / pressure / wind | MEDA derived data, one sol (PDS Atmospheres) | Historical Replay (at the rover) |

| Arrival preview | Perseverance raw images API | Observation (representative) |

## How it works

- `src/terrain.js` — loads the preprocessed DEM grid (or makes the synthetic one) and computes slope.
- `src/routing.js` — A* (8-connected) with a hard slope limit; Tobler hiking speed × suit factor; three route profiles; timed samples for the timeline.
- `src/environment.js` — time/position-dependent environment with provenance labels; dose integration.
- `src/astronaut.js` — data-driven gait (uphill / downhill / sampling), dust particles from τ, radiation ring + HUD warning. No wind “lean”: Martian dynamic pressure is ~1 Pa.
- `src/resources.js` — Civ-style resource badges for real places (`src/icons.js` generated by `scripts/gen_icons.cjs`).
- `src/stormsim.js` — planet-scale storm footprint fitted to the 2018 and 2022 storm timelines; per-base arrival, warning with sharing, and the shared hazard log. `src/dustwall.js` — the advancing dust front on the local map.
- `src/visor.js` — first-person helmet visor (**First person** button or **F**): compass tape with bearings to the next stop, the habitat and your EVA partner; EVA time left, dose rate and EVA dose, dust optical depth, air temperature, pressure and wind, each with its provenance pill; slope and grade underfoot; dust-front countdown and re-plan notices as visor alerts. Drag to look around; dust optical depth thickens the haze.
- `src/main.js` — three.js scene, comparison table, timeline, click-to-set destination.

## Hazard warnings and mid-EVA re-planning

Turn on a scenario event in the Plan tab and press play.

- **Dust front:** a front moving east at 25 m/s (the speed measured for the January 2022 storm at Jezero) is picked up by a proposed warning network: a habitat met station plus four stations on the 100 km Exploration Zone boundary (NASA EZ radius, ESA four-lander study). You can switch to a 30 km ring or the habitat station alone to see how much warning each buys. No such network exists on Mars today. On the first detection the timeline pauses and Marswalk computes, from the crew's current position, how long each way to shelter takes and whether the crew gets inside before the front arrives: head straight home, retrace your own tracks, or get into a pressurized rover staged on the route.
- **Solar particle event:** replays the May 2024 RAD event profile and offers the fastest ways to shelter.

- **Shelter, then home:** in the staged rover the crew waits until the front and its gusts have passed (or the dose rate drops), then drives home inside it.
- **Two-team broadcast:** tick *Second EVA team* in the Plan tab. A team working upwind sees the front first and puts it on the shared log; the dialog shows how many minutes that added.
- **Planet-wide sharing (Globe view):** replay the 2018 global storm or the January 2022 regional storm, or a typical northern flushing storm. The globe shows the dust spreading, each base's status (clear / watch / in the storm), the shared hazard log, and how much warning sharing buys each base. The storm model is a kinematic fit to dated observations (checked by `npm test`), not a forecast.

- **Radiation:** quiet dose is Curiosity's RAD level adjusted to local air pressure (Guo et al. 2015); a solar-event alert follows the RAD nowcast of Löwe et al. 2025, confirmed by 2 of 4 dose sensors. See docs/RESEARCH.md section 5.

Why this matters, with sources (Opportunity 2018, Jezero 2022, 2018 storm growth, Apollo 1972, RAD 2024): [docs/RESEARCH.md](docs/RESEARCH.md), sections 1 and 4.

## Limitations (deliberate)

MEDA is a single-point historical record, not weather for the whole map and not a forecast. RAD is measured at Gale, not Jezero, and route-to-route dose differences come only from EVA duration. No terrain shielding model. Obstacles are avoided by slope only: the 20 m CTX elevation model cannot resolve individual boulders, so rock hazard needs HiRISE-scale data (25 cm imagery, ~1 m elevation models) before a real traverse. Science-site positions in `public/data/sites.json` are placeholders until you place them on real targets. Walking speed, slope limit, stop durations and the radiation warning threshold are Assumptions (illustrative, not NASA flight rules).
